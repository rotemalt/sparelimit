sparelimit.

Don't waste your AI coding limit.

Sparelimit is a small program that runs on your own computer, next to Claude Code. It warns you before a conversation grows so big that you waste your limit, and it counts a saving only when an alert fired, you acted and a lower cost was measured afterwards.

Open source, nothing to pay, and nothing leaves your computer.

Try it in two minutes Follow along

Try it in two minutes.

You need Node 20 or newer and nothing else. I have only checked it on a Mac. Four lines:

git clone https://github.com/rotemalt/sparelimit.git
cd sparelimit
./install.sh
sparelimit replay --all

The last line reads the logs your coding agent already wrote on your disk and shows where your limit went, across every past session. There is nothing to set up: no account, no key, no sign-in.

To be warned while you work, run sparelimit init. It shows what it would change and changes nothing. sparelimit init --apply does it.

Sparelimit reads Claude Code fully. It reads Codex and Gemini CLI for cost only, and Cursor for tokens only. What each one gives you.

Not counted, and why

  • 72 ignored: you did not act on the alert, so nothing was saved
  • 1 stopped: work stopped after the alert; that is not counted as an effect of it
  • 18 no-data: the logs could not settle it (too little spending to compare, or the alert did not apply)
  • 2 pending: too recent to judge: the window after the alert is not over

Complaints received: 0 (GitHub issues titled "Complaint:", counted before the repository was public, when none could be filed yet), as of 2026-11-26.

I ignored most of the alerts. Sparelimit can only save you something when you act on it, and most of the time I did not. That is the real number, and it is why the saving is small.

Since then I added one short command, sparelimit act: it writes a handoff note and copies a prompt for a fresh conversation, so acting on a warning takes less work. I have not measured whether it changes how often I act.

The counted saving is labelled estimated because it compares two stretches of work that may not be alike; the ledger says so itself. Dollars are at list price: on a subscription, a saving is limit you kept, not money back. How every number is worked out. A counted saving is not proof that an alert caused it, and the rules are the author's own choices: what Sparelimit does not claim.

Questions.

What leaves my computer?

Nothing. Sparelimit reads logs that are already on your disk and writes its own notes to a folder on your disk. This website has no forms and no cookies (see Privacy).

Is it safe to let it near my AI tool?

Nothing is installed until you run sparelimit init --apply, and sparelimit init first shows what it would change. Sparelimit warns first and only stops at high thresholds. sparelimit allow turns the stops into warnings for a while.

How do I know the numbers are right?

Every figure is labelled exact, derived or estimated, and the methodology page says how each is worked out. I compared the totals with ccusage and CodeBurn on my own sessions: they differ by 0.004% and 0.03%.

How is this different from ccusage or CodeBurn?

Both are good and widely used. ccusage reports what you spent. CodeBurn also reports, and it can warn, stop and re-measure after a fix it applies. Sparelimit's difference is narrower than a list of features: it counts a saving only when an alert fired, you acted and a lower cost was measured afterwards, and it publishes the rules it judges by. If you only want a report, you may not need Sparelimit. A dated comparison, with links to what each project says about itself, is in docs/COMPARISON.md.

Do I need to be technical?

Today Sparelimit is a command-line program. If you use Claude Code, you can use it.
