sparelimit.

Privacy

Last changed 2026-11-26.

Sparelimit runs on your machine and sends nothing unless you switch it on. This page says what it reads, what it writes, what can leave your machine because of something you do, and what the website and the project see.

Who runs this

Rotem Alt publishes Sparelimit. Write to rotem.alt.verarail@gmail.com about anything on this page.

What the tool reads

  • The session logs your coding agent already writes on your disk (Claude Code, including the Desktop app's agent sessions; Codex CLI; Gemini CLI; Cursor's chat database; Antigravity in part). These logs can hold your prompts, your code, file paths and other people's information.
  • Account details your agent keeps locally, when you use the account views: a one-way hash of the account or organisation id, the organisation name and the plan type. The email address is never read.
  • The plan-usage record the Claude Desktop app keeps, when you ask for it.

What the tool writes, all on your machine

  • A folder ~/.sparelimit: your settings, the log of the warnings it showed, caches of numbers, session ids, project folder paths, tool names and warning messages. The caches hold no prompt text.
  • In a project folder, when the feature is used: .sparelimit-status.json, .sparelimit-handoff.md (a note for a fresh conversation, which can contain your last request), .sparelimit-muzzle.sh, .sparelimit-freeze.json, and report or picture files you ask for.
  • Entries in your agent's settings file, only if you run sparelimit init --apply. sparelimit init first shows them.
  • Before a handoff, a status file or an export carries your text, well-known shapes of passwords and keys are hidden. A secret with no known shape is not found.

The optional count

Off unless you say yes. Sparelimit asks once, after your first replay or weekly summary in a terminal (or when the ledger first shows a counted saving): yes, not now, or never. Not now is asked once more after 30 days. You can change your answer at any time with sparelimit count on or sparelimit count off.

If you say yes, Sparelimit downloads one tiny file from GitHub, once when you opt in and at most once a day when it counts a saving, so the author can see from GitHub's own download counter that people use it. Nothing about you or your work is sent to the author: no name, no path, no project, no time of work, and no identifier exists. GitHub sees your address, as it does for any download. The totals are public and approximate. Sparelimit works the same either way.

sparelimit count show prints the two file addresses. Your answer is kept in ~/.sparelimit/count.json.

What can leave your machine

Apart from the optional count, Sparelimit itself opens no connection to the internet. These things move information because you chose them:

  • Warnings that Sparelimit hands to your AI tool, answers it gives that tool through sparelimit mcp, and a handoff you paste: they become part of your conversation, and go to that tool's provider under its terms.
  • sparelimit sync writes a file of daily totals into a folder you choose. If another service copies that folder, that service moves the file. It holds totals, model names, account keys and the device name, never prompts or paths.
  • npm run update-pricing, a script you run by hand from the source, downloads a public price list from GitHub.
  • Installing or updating downloads files from GitHub or npm, under their terms.
  • Anything you post yourself, such as a weekly summary in an issue.

The local dashboard

sparelimit serve shows a page at 127.0.0.1, reachable from your own machine only. It refuses requests that another website starts. It can show prompt text and local paths, so do not share your screen or a screenshot of it.

The website

  • It has no forms, no cookies, no analytics and no third-party scripts or fonts.
  • It keeps one thing in your browser's local storage: whether you chose the bright or the dark theme. Clearing your browser data removes it.
  • It is served by a hosting company (GitHub Pages), which receives your IP address and request details as any web host does, under its own privacy statement.

The project on GitHub

Issues, discussions and pull requests are public. Do not put personal or private information in them. GitHub handles them under its own privacy statement. The author can see the repository's traffic totals, stars and watchers that GitHub shows to owners.

If you write to the author

Your email address and what you write are used to answer you, are not sold or shared, and are deleted when you ask, unless the law requires keeping them.

Your choices and rights

Everything the tool stores is on your machine: you can read it and delete it. sparelimit init --uninstall takes out what it added to your agent's settings, and saves a backup of the file first. For anything the author holds (emails you sent), write to rotem.alt.verarail@gmail.com to see, correct or delete it. Depending on where you live, you may also be able to complain to a data protection authority.
